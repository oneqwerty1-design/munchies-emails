We Need To Talk

Your Tolerance
Isn't Broken.
It's Just Math.

Nobody in this industry explains this honestly, so we're going to.

Remember your first gummy? You were on the couch, describing the ceiling to somebody who did not ask.

Now you take the same dose and text back within four minutes.

That's not a bad batch. That's tolerance, and it happens to everyone.

What's Actually Happening

THC works by binding to CB1 receptors in your brain. Use it consistently and those receptors do what any over-stimulated system does — they turn the volume down. Fewer receptors listening, weaker signal, smaller effect from the exact same dose.

It isn't damage. It's adaptation. Your body is annoyingly good at it.

Which is also why "just take more" is a losing strategy. Doubling a dose your receptors have already learned to ignore mostly just doubles what you spend to get the same shrug.

Option One

Take A Break

Stop for a stretch. Receptor sensitivity starts climbing back within a couple of days, and a few weeks off gets most people meaningfully closer to where they started.

Costs nothing. Downside: involves not being high.

Option Two

Change The Cannabinoid

Switch to something your receptors haven't spent months adjusting to. THCP binds to those same CB1 receptors far more tightly than THC does — in lab testing, roughly 33 times the binding affinity.

That number is affinity, not a promise it's 33 times stronger. But it does mean it still registers when regular THC has started feeling polite.

Same lock. Different key. Considerably firmer handshake.

We're Not Selling You
Anything Today.

We just think you should know why the thing that used to work stopped working. Most brands would rather you quietly bought more.

Monday, we'll have something for the option-two crowd.
